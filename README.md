# Ozhkaryveth

> The mountain wore a door around its neck. Nobody asked what it had locked away.

## A Chapel That Walks at Night

Ozhkaryveth is a chapel built upon a plateau of dark salt. Travelers find it at different distances from the same village. The villagers explain that the chapel walks during the night, choosing places where its bell will not be heard.

Its walls contain no bell tower. Its roof contains no opening. Still, a deep note is heard beneath the floor whenever someone kneels without knowing whom they intend to address.

The altar holds a stone pendant shaped like a door. A cord passes through its upper edge and disappears into the wall behind it. Every attempt to measure the cord produces the same result: the circumference of a mountain seen from a place where no mountain stands.

## The Pilgrim of Ash

A pilgrim arrived during a season when the village's fires cast white shadows. He wore a robe sewn from coarse gray cloth, and ash fell from its sleeves without gathering on the ground.

He asked to see the pendant. The chapel keeper brought a lamp, revealing a small keyhole in the stone door. The pilgrim bent toward it, and the keeper glimpsed a curved horn beneath his hood.

From inside the keyhole came the sound of a vast creature breathing. The pilgrim listened until his own breath matched its rhythm. Then he tied a knot in the cord and left through an opening that had not existed beside the altar.

The opening vanished after him. The knot remained, slowly tightening around a space that no finger could enter.

## The Village That Heard the Wrong Bell

That evening, the village heard a bell beneath every floor. Its note differed from the chapel's, but every listener recognized it as the sound they had been waiting for since childhood.

People searched their houses. Beneath one bed, they found a footprint made of salt. Beneath another, a small door had appeared in the grain of the wood. A third house contained nothing unusual, except that its occupants could no longer remember which wall faced the plateau.

The village sent a delegation to the chapel. They walked until dawn and discovered the building standing behind them, exactly where their own shadows had been cast at sunset.

The keeper received them with a lamp whose flame was white. He asked which bell they had brought. Nobody knew how to answer without first searching their hands.

## A Measurement of the Hidden Mountain

The delegation included a surveyor. He stretched a cord around the chapel and recorded its dimensions upon a slate. The measurements described a building with one room more than the keeper remembered.

The additional room appeared inside the altar. Its walls were made of dark salt, and its ceiling resembled the underside of a folded wing. At its center hung a stone door, much larger than the pendant.

The surveyor drew the room. His slate grew heavy enough to sink into the plateau. He left it where it fell, covering the drawing with his coat.

By morning, the coat lay upon bare salt. The slate was gone. Its measurements appeared as pale lines across the mountain that the village had never been able to see.

## The Words inside the Knot

**Ozh — kar — yveth — athuun.**

These sounds are heard when the cord's knot tightens. Each resembles a word spoken by someone standing on the far side of a thick stone wall.

The keeper tried to loosen the knot. The first sound became clear, but the chapel's floor began to rise beneath his feet. Through a narrow gap, he saw scales moving around a great curved surface. A door hung against those scales, swinging from a cord thicker than the chapel itself.

He withdrew his hands. The knot closed again, and the floor settled. For several nights, the altar cast the shadow of a mountain wearing something bright around its neck.

The keeper covered the pendant with cloth. The cloth turned white wherever it touched the keyhole.

## The Prayer without an Address

> Where does the chapel go when the plateau sleeps?
>
> Where the cord has not yet reached its knot.
>
> Who breathes behind the stone door?
>
> The one who mistook the mountain for a room.
>
> Why does the village hear another bell?
>
> Because someone has begun kneeling beneath its houses.

The delegation heard these answers during a night spent beside the altar. The keeper slept throughout the exchange. In the morning, he asked whether the pilgrim had returned.

No pilgrim had been seen. A thin layer of ash covered the lamp, though its white flame remained visible through the gray.

## The Plateau beyond the Last House

The village has stopped recording the chapel's distance. Travelers still arrive with maps, but leave them folded before entering the plateau.

The stone pendant remains upon the altar. The knot continues tightening. Sometimes the chapel stands close enough for its windows to reflect the village's houses; sometimes those reflections contain doors beneath the beds.

Ozhkaryveth moves when nobody watches the cord. Far beyond the last house, an unseen mountain turns in its sleep, carrying a room that has not decided which side of its door is outside.
